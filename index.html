01 · Source

About

portrait.jpg

I started writing code in 2004, with C++ in high school, and I've never drifted far from it, even while trying on other careers. I once joined a team of biologists whose job was to read journal articles and record data points by hand. I spent the first couple of weeks falling behind everyone, because I was writing a scraper instead. Once it worked, it pulled the data about 50,000 times faster.

Working only in tech burned me out a bit, so I've always paired it with another field: cybersecurity for a while, then environmental science with a focus on climate, and eventually back to biology for a master's in bioinformatics and genomics. I'm an engineer first, but knowing the science makes me more useful to the researchers generating the data.

Today I work at EMSL, where I get to move science data around all day. AI is a core part of my daily work, and I enjoy testing bleeding-edge tools to see what else we can automate. That leaves more of my time for what I'm best at: architecture, schemas, APIs, and tracking down the edge case everyone else missed, usually by actually reading the logs.

03 · Transform

Projects

Sole developer & architect

Data automation for AMP2

The data system behind the Anaerobic Microbial Phenotyping Platform (AMP2), EMSL's robotic lab for studying microbes that live without oxygen. It coordinates runs across 18 instruments, collects what they produce, runs analysis and AI models on the results, and routes everything to long-term storage. It's in its pilot phase today, and it's being designed to scale up to M2PC, a planned facility with more than 30,000 sq ft of instrumentation that will move terabytes of data every day.

  • 18 instruments in pilot
  • M2PC: 30,000+ sq ft
  • Terabytes per day
  • AI models in the loop

Lead developer

Peta-scale data storage

Lead developer on two systems that hold EMSL's scientific data: a lakehouse that makes it queryable, and the catalog behind the long-term archive that preserves it. The lakehouse runs on a 3.5 PB VAST flash tier, and the archive tier is over 70 PB.

  • 3.5 PB VAST flash tier
  • 70+ PB archive tier
  • Lakehouse
  • Archive catalog
